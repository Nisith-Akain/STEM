# Neutralization Reactions

A **neutralisation reaction** is when an acid reacts with a base and cancels it out, making a **salt** and **water**. The H⁺ ions from the acid join with the OH⁻ ions from the base to make water:

```latex
H^+ + OH^- \rightarrow H_2O
```

- Acids have a pH below 7, bases above 7. A complete neutralisation ends at pH 7 (neutral).
- Neutralisation reactions give out heat (they are **exothermic**).
- A base that dissolves in water is called an **alkali** (e.g. sodium hydroxide).

## 1. Acid + metal hydroxide (base)

**acid + metal hydroxide → salt + water**

- Example: hydrochloric acid + sodium hydroxide → sodium chloride + water
- Symbol equation: HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H₂O
- What you see: no visible change, but the mixture gets warmer. Use an indicator to see the colour change.

## 2. Acid + metal oxide

**acid + metal oxide → salt + water**

- Example: sulfuric acid + copper oxide → copper sulfate + water
- Symbol equation: H₂SO₄ + CuO → CuSO₄ + H₂O
- What you see: the black copper oxide powder disappears and the solution turns blue.

## 3. Acid + metal carbonate

**acid + metal carbonate → salt + water + carbon dioxide**

- Example: hydrochloric acid + calcium carbonate → calcium chloride + water + carbon dioxide
- Symbol equation: 2HCl + CaCO₃ → CaCl₂ + H₂O + CO₂
- What you see: fizzing (bubbles of CO₂) and the solid disappears.
- Test for CO₂: bubble it through **limewater**, which turns **milky/cloudy**.

## 4. Acid + metal hydrogen carbonate

**acid + metal hydrogen carbonate → salt + water + carbon dioxide**

- Example: hydrochloric acid + sodium hydrogen carbonate (baking soda) → sodium chloride + water + carbon dioxide
- Symbol equation: HCl + NaHCO₃ → NaCl + H₂O + CO₂
- What you see: lots of fizzing. Same products as a carbonate, but only one acid is needed for each NaHCO₃.

## Bonus: acid + metal (not neutralisation)

**acid + metal → salt + hydrogen**

- Example: hydrochloric acid + magnesium → magnesium chloride + hydrogen
- Symbol equation: 2HCl + Mg → MgCl₂ + H₂
- Test for H₂: a lit splint makes a **squeaky pop**.
- This isn't neutralisation (a metal isn't a base), but it's often taught alongside it.

## Naming the salt

The first part of the salt's name comes from the **metal**, the second part from the **acid**:

| Acid | Formula | Salt ending | Example salt |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hydrochloric acid | HCl | -chloride | sodium chloride, NaCl |
| Sulfuric acid | H₂SO₄ | -sulfate | copper sulfate, CuSO₄ |
| Nitric acid | HNO₃ | -nitrate | potassium nitrate, KNO₃ |

## Cheat sheet

| Reaction | Products | What you see | Gas test |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Acid + metal hydroxide | salt + water | gets warm, no fizzing | — |
| Acid + metal oxide | salt + water | solid dissolves, solution may change colour | — |
| Acid + metal carbonate | salt + water + CO₂ | fizzing | CO₂ turns limewater milky |
| Acid + metal hydrogen carbonate | salt + water + CO₂ | fizzing | CO₂ turns limewater milky |
| Acid + metal | salt + hydrogen | fizzing, metal disappears | H₂ gives a squeaky pop |

**Memory tricks:**

- **MASH**: Metal + Acid → Salt + Hydrogen
- **CASWC**: Carbonate + Acid → Salt + Water + Carbon dioxide
- Hydroxides and oxides only ever give **salt + water**.
- Salt name = metal first + acid ending (-chloride, -sulfate, -nitrate).

## Learn more with PhET

- [pH Scale](https://phet.colorado.edu/en/simulations/ph-scale): test everyday liquids and see the balance of H⁺ (H₃O⁺) and OH⁻ ions change with pH.
- [Acid-Base Solutions](https://phet.colorado.edu/en/simulations/acid-base-solutions): measure pH with paper or a probe and compare strong and weak acids and bases.

Neither sim mixes an acid with a base to show the full reaction, but both show the H⁺ and OH⁻ ions that neutralisation is all about.
